Met the Spend Target and Still Lost? why a sign-up bonus may not pay

The spending target met, and no points

You opened a credit card for the sign-up bonus. Spend a set amount in the first three months and get the points.

You spent it. The points did not arrive.

Eight more weeks, then a different answer

  1. You applied through an advert or a referral link offering a bonus.
  2. The condition was a spending total within a set number of days.
  3. You met it, with room to spare.
  4. The stated waiting period passed. Nothing arrived.
  5. You called. You were told to wait a further eight weeks.
  6. You called again. You were told the offer did not apply to your application.
  7. Nobody can tell you which offer was attached to your account.

Why an offer attaches at opening or not at all

The offer and the account are recorded separately. Whether a specific offer attached to your application depends on how you applied, which link you used, and what the system recorded at the time.

If it did not attach, there is often no way to prove it should have. The advert you saw is gone. The link is gone. The terms may have changed.

Meanwhile the spending is done. You have held up your side of a bargain the other party may have no record of.

The check: spend since opening against the target

Two things you already have: what the offer required, and what you actually spent. Nobody puts them side by side because nobody is watching the deadline except you.

Where Plenee fits

This is one of the cleanest matches between what goes wrong and what Plenee can see.

  • It already knows your spending. Spend on a new card, from the day it opened, is a

running total Plenee holds without being asked.

  • It can track the target. $2,400 of $3,000 with 31 days left is a useful sentence.

Nobody sends it to you, and missing the target by a small margin is a common way to lose a bonus entirely.

  • It can watch for the reward arriving. A bonus that was due and has not landed is an

expected payment that has not arrived — the same shape as the car refund in GAP and Warranty Refunds: what you are owed when a car loan ends early, and just as invisible.

  • It keeps the dates. Account opened, target met, deadline passed. That is the whole

complaint, in three dates.

Evidencing a bonus you earned

  • Screenshot the offer when you apply. This is the single thing that decides these

disputes, and it takes five seconds.

  • Ask which offer code is attached to your account, and ask for it in writing.
  • Give the exact dates and amounts. "I spent $3,140 between the 4th and the 62nd day"

is far stronger than saying you met it.

  • Complain formally if refused. These are resolved in the customer's favor often

enough to be worth the twenty minutes.

How often the complaint is simply a bonus

Complaints about advertised or promotional terms not being honored are about one in thirty-two credit card complaints.1 Almost the entire category is a single story: a bonus that was earned and not paid.2
